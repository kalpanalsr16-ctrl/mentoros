**Product:** MentorOS

**Version:** 1.0

**Status:** Draft

---

# Purpose

This document defines the primary users of MentorOS.

The goal is not simply to understand who uses the product, but how they think, learn, struggle, and interact with AI.

Every product decision, agent behavior, and personalization strategy should be informed by these personas.

---

# Primary User Groups

MentorOS is designed for four primary user groups:

1. Primary School Students (Grades 1–7)
2. High School Students (Grades 8–12)
3. Parents
4. Teachers (Future Release)

---

# Persona 1 — Curious Explorer

## Profile

Age: 7–10 years

Grade: 2–5

Learning Stage: Foundation

Technology Comfort: High

Reading Ability: Developing

Primary Device: Tablet or Mobile

---

## Goals

• Understand concepts through stories

• Learn while having fun

• Build confidence

• Ask unlimited questions

---

## Challenges

• Easily distracted

• Limited attention span

• Struggles with abstract concepts

• Hesitates to ask questions in class

---

## Learning Style

Prefers:

✓ Stories

✓ Real-life examples

✓ Visual explanations

✓ Voice conversations

✓ Encouragement

---

## AI Expectations

"I want someone who explains patiently."

---

## Product Expectations

MentorOS should:

- Speak slowly

- Use simple words

- Avoid technical terminology

- Celebrate small achievements

- Encourage curiosity

---

## Success Criteria

The student should:

- Enjoy learning

- Ask more questions

- Build conceptual understanding

- Feel confident solving problems independently

---

# Persona 2 — Concept Builder

## Profile

Age: 11–14

Grades: 6–8

Learning Stage: Intermediate

Technology Comfort: Very High

Primary Device: Laptop / Tablet

---

## Goals

- Understand concepts deeply

- Improve school performance

- Build problem-solving skills

---

## Challenges

- Memorizes instead of understanding

- Confuses similar concepts

- Loses confidence after repeated mistakes

---

## Learning Style

Prefers:

✓ Step-by-step explanations

✓ Examples

✓ Practice

✓ Interactive conversations

---

## AI Expectations

"Explain until I understand."

---

## Product Expectations

MentorOS should:

- Break difficult concepts into smaller ideas

- Ask follow-up questions

- Identify misconceptions

- Adapt explanations

---

## Success Criteria

Student becomes comfortable solving unfamiliar questions.

---

# Persona 3 — Exam Achiever

## Profile

Age: 15–18

Grades: 9–12

Preparing for Board Exams

Technology Comfort: High

---

## Goals

- Score higher marks

- Master difficult concepts

- Prepare efficiently

- Practice exam-style questions

---

## Challenges

- Time pressure

- Large syllabus

- Fear of difficult topics

- Exam anxiety

---

## Learning Style

Prefers:

✓ Fast explanations

✓ Formula summaries

✓ Practice papers

✓ Previous year questions

✓ Revision plans

---

## AI Expectations

"I don't want the answer.

I want to know how to solve it."

---

## Product Expectations

MentorOS should:

- Detect weak topics

- Suggest revision

- Generate adaptive practice

- Track progress

- Recommend next concepts

---

## Success Criteria

Student improves confidence and examination performance.

---

# Persona 4 — Parent

## Goals

- Monitor learning progress

- Identify weak concepts

- Encourage consistent study

---

## Challenges

- Limited time

- Unsure how to help

- Cannot monitor daily learning

---

## Expectations

Parents expect MentorOS to provide:

- Weekly summaries

- Learning insights

- Recommendations

- Improvement trends

---

# Persona 5 — Teacher (Future)

## Goals

- Understand class performance

- Identify difficult concepts

- Track student progress

---

## Expectations

Teachers should eventually receive:

- Class analytics

- Learning reports

- Weak concept dashboards

- AI-generated teaching insights

---

# Personalization Dimensions

MentorOS should continuously personalize based on:

## Academic

- Grade

- Subject

- Concept mastery

- Previous mistakes

- Learning speed

---

## Behavioral

- Attention span

- Confidence

- Curiosity

- Question frequency

- Practice consistency

---

## Interaction

- Voice vs Text

- Preferred explanation style

- Session duration

- Preferred difficulty level

---

## Emotional

The platform should detect when learners are:

- Confused

- Frustrated

- Curious

- Confident

- Bored

The teaching strategy should adapt accordingly.

---

# Accessibility

MentorOS should support learners with different needs by providing:

- Voice interaction

- Adjustable reading complexity

- Slower explanations

- Multiple explanation styles

Future versions may include accessibility features for learners with dyslexia, ADHD, or visual impairments.

---

# Product Design Implications

These personas influence:

- Router Agent decisions

- Explanation style

- Practice generation

- Hint strategy

- Assessment difficulty

- Voice tone

- Memory personalization

Every AI interaction should feel tailored to the learner rather than generic.

---

# Success Definition

A successful MentorOS experience means:

The learner feels understood.

The learner gains confidence.

The learner develops conceptual understanding.

The learner chooses to return because learning feels engaging and personalized.
